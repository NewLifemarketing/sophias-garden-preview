A Canadian manufacturer

Sophia's Garden (Artisan) Inc. operates from 600 Montreal St. in Thunder Bay, Ontario, under licences issued by Health Canada. Manufacturing here rather than contracting it out is what keeps formulation and production in the same building.

From formulation to released lot

  1. 1

    Incoming material

    Cannabinoid inputs and food ingredients are received under the record-keeping the licence requires.

  2. 2

    Extract preparation

    The cannabinoid is built into the cocoa butter phase as a controlled step of its own.

  3. 3

    Tempering and moulding

    The batch is tempered and moulded into individual units under controlled temperature and humidity.

  4. 4

    Laboratory testing

    A sample from the lot goes to a licensed analytical laboratory.

  5. 5

    Quality assurance release

    A quality assurance person confirms the lot meets requirements. Product does not ship on commercial judgement alone.

  6. 6

    Packaging and distribution

    Packaged to the labelling rules, then into the provincial system.

Small batch, by choice

We work in small batches. In edible manufacturing that means each production run is small enough to be checked closely and adjusted, rather than large enough that problems are discovered after the fact.

Confectionery discipline, plus cannabis regulation

Chocolate manufacturing has its own standards — temperature control, humidity control, sanitation, and the handling practices any food producer is held to. Cannabis regulation is layered on top of that, not instead of it, and both apply at every step.

The people who develop the formulation are in the same building as the people who make it. That is most of what “craft” means here.

Why it stays here

Northwestern Ontario is not the obvious place to build a cannabinoid chocolate lab. It is where the company is from, and keeping manufacturing local is a deliberate part of what the business is.
