Cocoa butter cannabinoid extracts, explained

The extract is where our formulation work starts. What a cocoa butter cannabinoid extract is, why we build one rather than buying a finished distillate and stirring it in, and what it solves.

The most consequential decision in an edible formulation is made before any chocolate exists. It is the decision about what form the cannabinoid arrives in, and how it is introduced.

Distillate as a starting point

Cannabis distillate is a refined, concentrated cannabinoid preparation. It is the common raw material for edible manufacturing, it is highly consistent, and it is where a great many products begin and end — the distillate is dosed into a batch and the batch is moulded.

That approach works. It also leaves the formulator dependent on mixing to achieve distribution, which as discussed elsewhere is the weakest link in a low-dose product.

Building the extract into the fat phase

The alternative is to incorporate the cannabinoid into the cocoa butter itself, producing a cocoa butter cannabinoid extract that is then used as an ingredient in the chocolate. The active is dispersed in the fat phase before that fat becomes part of a confection.

The advantage is structural rather than incremental. Instead of asking a mixing step to distribute a small amount of oil through a large volume of chocolate, you are asking it to distribute an already-homogeneous fat through a fat system it is chemically compatible with. The hard part is moved upstream, into a smaller and more controllable volume.

What it demands

It also means the extract preparation becomes its own controlled process, with its own variables: temperature, incorporation method, holding conditions, and verification that the extract itself is uniform before it goes anywhere near a batch of chocolate.

In other words it adds a manufacturing step in order to remove a source of variability. That is usually a good trade in production, and it is the specific trade our formats depend on.

Why we talk about it

Extract work is not visible in a finished product and it is not something a package can communicate. It is, however, the part of the business that took the longest to develop and the part that determines whether a 1 mg claim is dependable.

The strategy we work to describes SGA as a premium edible and extract lab. This is the extract half of that sentence, and it is a genuine engineering capability rather than a description of an ingredient we buy.
