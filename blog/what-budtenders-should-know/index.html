What budtenders should know about low-dose chocolate formats

Written for the counter. What actually distinguishes our two formats, what the numbers on the package mean, and what can and cannot be said to a customer.

Budtenders influence somewhere between forty and seventy percent of purchasing decisions in Canada. That makes retail staff the most important audience a producer has, and it makes a clear, accurate brief more valuable than any amount of advertising.

This one is written for the counter.

The two formats, plainly

Both products are chocolates formulated at 1 mg of each of two cannabinoids per unit, with ten units in a pack. That is the shared architecture. The difference is the second cannabinoid and the flavour system built around it.

MICRO ZEN is a milk chocolate pairing THC with CBD in a balanced 1:1 arrangement. MICRO ZEST is a yuzu chocolate pairing THC with CBG — a less common cannabinoid — in the same balanced arrangement. Yuzu is a Japanese citrus, sharp and aromatic, chosen to carry flavour without additional sweetness.

What the numbers mean

One milligram per unit is a low dose by design, and the format is built to make that number dependable. Ten units per pack means the increments are small and consistent. For a customer asking about control and predictability, that is the honest answer: this is a measured format.

It is worth knowing why low dose is technically harder rather than easier. Manufacturing tolerance is proportional, so a smaller declared figure demands a tighter process to hit the same accuracy. That is a real differentiator and it is defensible, because every lot is verified by laboratory analysis.

What can be said

You can describe what is in the product and how much. You can describe the format — chocolate, unit size, pack count. You can describe how it is made, including the cocoa butter extract work and why it supports even distribution. You can describe where it is manufactured, and that it is laboratory tested per lot.

What cannot be said

Not what it does. No description of effects, no suggestion of occasions, nothing about how a customer might feel. That is a legal boundary for the producer and a sensible one for a store — staff who repeat claims that go beyond the documentation create risk for the business they work in.

If a customer asks a question that can only be answered with an effects claim, the accurate response is about the format: this is a low, measured dose in a consistent unit, and here is what the package declares.

Where the material lives

Product knowledge guides, cannabinoid education cards, comparison charts and counter cheat sheets are in the budtender portal. Registration is with a work email on a business domain, and Ontario’s rule applies throughout: education only, no financial incentives of any kind.
