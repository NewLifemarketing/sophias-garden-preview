What a Health Canada licence requires of a processor

“Health Canada licensed” appears on a great deal of packaging and is rarely explained. What the licence actually authorises, and the obligations that come with holding one.

Federal licensing is the foundation everything else rests on, and it is more specific than the phrase on a package suggests. A licence is not a one-off approval or a general credential. It authorises particular activities at a particular site, and it carries continuing obligations.

Licence classes

The Cannabis Act and its regulations set out several classes. Processing licences authorise activities such as extraction, manufacturing, packaging and labelling. Cultivation licences authorise growing. There are separate authorisations covering sale, including for medical purposes, and micro-class licences for smaller-scale operations.

A company may hold more than one class. Sophia’s Garden (Artisan) Inc. holds Standard Cultivation, Standard Processing, and Sales for Medical Purpose licences. Our activity is manufacturing: we are an edible and extract producer.

Site-specific by design

A licence attaches to a physical address. It authorises activities at that site, and the physical and security characteristics of the site form part of what was assessed. Operations cannot simply be relocated, and changes to a licensed site are themselves regulated.

Security and record-keeping

Licensed sites operate under physical security requirements covering controlled access, monitoring and restrictions on who may enter which areas. Personnel in certain roles require security clearance. Record-keeping is continuous rather than periodic, and traceability is maintained across every lot.

Good production practices

Production is governed by good production practices covering sanitation, equipment, premises, personnel hygiene and the systems that prevent contamination or mix-ups. For an edible manufacturer these sit alongside food-manufacturing discipline rather than replacing it — chocolate production has its own standards for temperature, humidity and handling, and both apply.

Testing and release

Product is tested before release, and a quality assurance person — a defined role under the regulations — is responsible for confirming a lot meets requirements before it goes anywhere. Product does not ship on commercial judgement alone.

Packaging, labelling and promotion

The same framework governs how a product may be packaged, labelled and discussed. Plain-packaging rules limit branding. Promotion rules restrict what a licence holder may say publicly and to whom. These are not adjacent to the licence — they are part of operating under one, and they are the reason this website reads the way it does.
